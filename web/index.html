01 · The strategic game

Under strong moderation, staying quiet is the best reply.

Player 1, the content sharer, chooses S (spread) or N (stay quiet). Player 2, the platform, chooses W (weak) or M (strong moderation). They move simultaneously. Each cell shows (u₁, u₂).

  • Normal-form game
  • Best response
  • Strict dominance
  • Nash equilibrium
  • Pareto efficiency
  • 5Underline = best response. The payoff is the highest that player can get against the other's choice (BR₁ for the sharer, BR₂ for the platform).
  • DominantDominant strategy. It pays more than the alternative whatever the opponent does.
  • Nash equilibrium. Both payoffs in the cell are best responses, so neither player gains by switching alone.

    02 · Diffusion of a story

    How far a story gets depends on how early it is cut off.

    Each step, the people already reaching others pass the story to a share of those who haven't seen it. Moderation m removes a fraction of that pull, so the effective rate is β(1 − m). The grey lines are four fixed policies; the highlighted line is yours.

    • Logistic growth
    • SIR with debunking
    • Moderation as a damping parameter
    • Comparative statics

    People exposed to the story at each step, for four fixed moderation policies and for the current setting.

    03 · The tipping point

    Past one level of moderation, spreading stops paying.

    Here the payoffs are not fixed. They are built from the simulation: the sharer's reward grows with β, the platform's damage with how far the story reached, and both pay for moderation. Move m and watch the equilibrium change.

    • Endogenous payoffs
    • Threshold strategy
    • Equilibrium shift

    The sharer's payoff from spreading and from staying quiet as moderation rises from zero to one. The lines cross at the tipping point.
